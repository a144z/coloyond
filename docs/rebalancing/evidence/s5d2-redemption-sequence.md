# S5D2 — split in-kind redemption sequence sensitivity

**Status:** candidate integer-rounding evidence only. This does not choose a redemption rule, establish holder fairness, or prove Anchor/SPL CPI behavior.

## Model

`tools/rebalancing/etf-economics/redemption_sequence_sensitivity.py` applies the draft ETF v1 formula separately at each step: for a partial burn, each asset pays `floor(burn × current reserve / current share supply)` base units, then custody and share supply are reduced before the next burn. A final burn of all remaining shares returns all remaining custody units exactly, as specified by the draft full-exit rule. All arithmetic is integer-exact; inputs are bounded to `u64` reserves and share supply. The model excludes transaction fees, Token-2022 extensions, account/CPI constraints and actual execution ordering.

The fixed grid has 33 cases: three synthetic reserve profiles crossed with eleven schedules covering one-shot 50%, two/three/ten-way 50% splits, unequal 50% ordering, one-shot/three-way 90%, one-shot/three-way 99.9999%, and a three-part sequence ending with exact full exit. Asset order and decimals are USDC/BTC/SOL at 6/8/9. The checked-in [replay table](../../../tools/rebalancing/etf-economics/redemption_sequence_results.csv) is regenerated by running the script; its SHA-256 is `b34d251b11b020728ec3f84ace6acc929955223fc925ccf7be0395b1f94dafe9`.

## Result

Every simulated split partial payout was at or below the corresponding one-shot floor payout for the same total share burn in this finite grid. The largest difference was 4 base units for an asset across the 33 cases; the ten-way half-redemption schedule produced the largest shortfalls. Exactly divisible reserve profiles matched one-shot output, and the final full-exit step returned every remaining base unit. This is evidence of bounded integer-floor effects in these scenarios, not a proof over all inputs or an economic materiality threshold.

Eight focused standard-library tests cover reserve conservation at each step, no overdraw, one-shot comparison, exact divisibility, full exit, `u64` boundaries, deterministic grid coverage and invalid-input rejection. These checks do not validate deployed contract arithmetic or establish that split redemptions should be restricted. S5A retains ownership of the final redemption policy and S5E must prove the selected formula against actual SPL CPI balances.
