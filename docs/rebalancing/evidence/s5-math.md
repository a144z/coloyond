# ETF v1 S5B math work log

**Status:** implementation in progress; S5B is not accepted and S5E remains blocked on review and remaining evidence.

## Current math profile candidate

- Pure `no_std` Rust crate with no new dependencies; no Anchor, token, RPC, oracle parser, wallet, or scheduler coupling.
- Two or three assets; target weights sum to 10,000 bps and each weight is at least 100 bps.
- Mint decimals are limited to 0–9; positive price exponents are rejected and negative price exponents are bounded to `[-17, 0]`.
- Oracle-marked values are normalized to integer `10^-12 USD` units, rounding down. Nonzero values below that resolution fail closed. The scale and bounds are a candidate selected by S5B implementation, pending independent review and contract freeze.
- Total-variation target deviation is projected to basis points with ceiling rounding. Trade direction compares the exact rational deviations using a 256-bit cross product, rather than rounded display bps.
- Exact-in weighted-mean quote permits at most 30% of input reserve and 30% of output reserve. Curve input is floored after fee; the gross input, including retained fee, remains in custody. Quote output is rounded down.
- The current working fixed-point power candidate uses Q96 arithmetic with conservative intermediate rounding. The permitted domain follows from the 30% input cap and 100 bps minimum weight; unsupported values and arithmetic overflow fail closed. This precision change is under validation and does not yet close S5B.

## Quote conservatism argument and independent review

For a supported exact-in swap, the gross input is at most 30% of the input reserve and the fee-adjusted input is no larger than gross. Therefore the exact reserve ratio `B_in / (B_in + net_in)` is at least `10/13`. The weight ratio is at most 99 because weights sum to 10,000 bps and every weight is at least 100 bps. Thus `-ln(base) × weight_in / weight_out < -ln(10/13) × 99 < 26`, the documented exponential domain.

The implementation rounds the reserve ratio up, the lower bound for `-ln(base)` down, and the exponent down. Its atanh-series terms round down and the omitted terms are nonnegative. For `exp(-z)`, it reduces `z` by flooring division by 256, evaluates the alternating Taylor series through even degree 12 on an argument below 0.102, and rounds positive terms and each of eight squarings up. These directions produce an upper bound for the curve power; subtracting from one and multiplying the output reserve with floor rounding produces an output no larger than the exact weighted-curve result. Because gross input is retained while only fee-adjusted input is priced, ideal invariant nondecrease follows for exact custody deltas. This is a mathematical argument for the bounded quote construction, not a substitute for broader implementation/property evidence or on-chain custody tests.

An independent read-only code review found no arithmetic unsoundness in the inspected quote/deviation paths and agreed the conservative-rounding argument is plausible over this domain. Since that review, generated coverage has been expanded: exact deviation vectors now cover two and three assets; 256 generated three-asset swaps span varied oracle marks and mint decimals while checking pairwise invariant nondecrease with retained fee plus same-snapshot drift improvement; 240 two-asset cases check the exact integer invariant at the maximum 99:1 weight ratio; 2,976 three-asset quotes cover the minimum-weight boundary; and 4,096 full-width two-asset samples compare against an exact integer custody-invariant bound. The new fixed-array share module implements the frozen initial NAV issuance, proportional deposit/refund, and in-kind redemption formulas; six integration tests cover 256 generated two/three-asset transitions and per-asset backing monotonicity under integer rounding. This is pure-math accounting evidence only, not token CPI/custody evidence. Remaining gaps include exhaustive fixed-point error and weighted-invariant coverage over every supported integer state, candidate-constant review, target-runtime compute, and SBF custody/share reconciliation. The Python standard-library generators make the selected math vectors reproducible; the focused share transition table is generated deterministically in Rust and independently checked against the frozen integer formulas.

Seven additional boundary tests now exercise pure share accounting near the full `u64` range: initial issuance reaches exactly `u64::MAX` shares and rejects the next issuance quantum; a near-maximum two-asset proportional deposit succeeds with ceil-consumption remainders, exact refunds, safe post-reserve/supply values and holder-backing monotonicity; a three-asset deposit near `u64::MAX` checks nonzero ceil remainders and refunds for all assets, exact custody/share-supply bounds, and that immediately redeeming its minted shares returns no more of each asset than contributed, followed by partial redemption with floor outputs and remaining-holder backing; isolated reserve and share-supply overflow transitions reject; a two-asset partial redemption floors outputs while preserving residual backing; two extreme partial burns of 1 and `u64::MAX - 1` check exact floors, monotonic outputs and residual backing; and full redemption returns maximum custody exactly. ## Newly identified blocking quote-precision case

An independent exact-integer review found a material underquote outside the earlier sampled input sizes. The custody-valid boundary is input reserve `u64::MAX - 1_000`, output reserve `u64::MAX`, weights `[9_900, 100]`, zero fee, with inputs 1, 100 and 1,000. Exact invariant-preserving floors are 99, 9,899 and 98,999; the prior Q64 path returned 0, 7,936 and 97,024. The initial probe using both reserves at `u64::MAX` was itself invalid for positive input because it overflowed input custody; it is not used as evidence. The Q64 logarithm could floor the tiny negative-log magnitude to zero, and its exponential path also discarded low bits during argument reduction. The working Q96 candidate now has Rust regressions against the exact floors and stays within one unit on these three vectors, without overpayment. A separate read-only arbitrary-precision prototype matched exact floors across 14,704 checked-in two/three-asset quote rows and 51,183 additional reserve/weight grid cases, and reported no overpayment and at most one unit shortfall; those prototype samples are supporting review, not a committed reproducible proof or SBF result. A checked-in arbitrary-precision generator and Rust regression now cover 297 unique near-`u64` tiny-input cases across all 99 valid two-asset weight steps and three tiny inputs. The weights cover low and high input-weight ratios; all cases use the same custody-valid asymmetric reserve anchor. Quotes never exceed the exact invariant floor and differ by at most one unit. Exact floors of zero or one may fail closed as `NoOutput`, consistent with the bounded one-unit dust allowance. All checked-in Rust math tests pass with Q96, including the 4,096 two-asset, 1,782 near-`u64` two-asset, 10,608 three-asset full-width quote corpora and 13,860-case exhaustive small-reserve sweep. Those corpora remain finite samples. The quantitative source-level interval proof is now separately documented and independently reviewed; target-runtime compute, broader full-boundary evidence, and product-level invariant/custody obligations remain required. Do not accept S5B or enable S5E until those remaining obligations pass; do not weaken dust, custody or invariant guards.
